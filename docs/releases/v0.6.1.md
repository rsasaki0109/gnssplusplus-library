# libgnss++ v0.6.1

A maintenance release for PPP. Static PPP with SSR corrections now uses
the carrier phase throughout (it had been dropped at an outlier gate for
most of a run when the predicted receiver clock was more than 10 m off),
`--dcb` works with real Bias-SINEX files, PPP with precise products no
longer carries the P1-C1 code bias on receivers that report C1W, and SSR
runs no longer mix uncorrected or single-frequency satellites with the
others. PPP with precise products gains the antenna phase centre
variations and a set of fixes found on 24 h station-days checked against
EPN daily coordinates, where it now ends within 1 to 2 cm. Several of
these fixes change default PPP results; they are listed below. The library/CPack/CMake package version is
0.6.1.

## Fixes

- **Static PPP keeps the carrier phase.** Once converged, the pre-fit gate
  tested each phase residual against 10 m. That residual holds the error of
  the predicted receiver clock, common to all satellites: 10 to 14 m on the
  Kamakura JPL GDGPS hours, where the phase of every satellite was dropped in
  about 3000 of 3599 epochs. The gate now tests the phase against the code
  row of the same satellite, and a new ambiguity is seeded from phase minus
  code instead of the predicted range.
- **SSR runs leave out a satellite tracked on one frequency** (L2 not yet
  acquired after rising), as runs with precise products already did. Such a
  row has no ionospheric delay model and stepped by 14 m when the second
  frequency arrived.
- **`gnss ppp --dcb` reads real Bias-SINEX OSB files.** An OSB row names one
  observable and leaves the second column blank; the reader dropped those
  rows, so a CODE or CAS OSB file loaded nothing. PPP now also looks a bias
  up by the observation's own RINEX code, so `C1C` / `C1W` and `C2W` / `C2L`
  each take their own bias. Receiver rows of a multi-GNSS file are no
  longer taken for satellite rows, and a multi-day file gives each day its
  own values.
- **PPP with precise products takes the GPS `C1W` range.** Precise GPS clocks
  (SP3 / CLK) refer to the P(Y) codes. A receiver that reports both `C1C`
  and `C1W` had its `C1C` range used, off by the satellite's P1-C1 bias.
  Without SSR and without `--dcb`, the `C1W` range is now used when the
  epoch has one; the carrier phase stays on L1 C/A.
- **RTCM SSR leaves out satellites the stream does not correct.** With
  `--ssr-rtcm` a satellite without a correction at an epoch was used on its
  broadcast orbit and clock. It is now left out, as it already was for HAS
  SIS and PPP-B2b.
- **Antenna phase centre variations are applied** (`--antex`, ionosphere-free
  PPP): the receiver antenna's against the zenith angle and the satellite
  antenna's against the nadir angle. Only the offsets were used; the 24 h
  height error of four stations went from 23 to 9 mm on average.
- **Receiver antennas calibrated for GPS and GLONASS only** (all of
  `igs14.atx`): Galileo and BeiDou signals had no phase centre offset at
  all, which put a Galileo-only or BeiDou-only 24 h solution 0.10 m high on
  a choke ring. A signal without its own entry takes the GPS one on the same
  carrier, else GPS L1 or L2 by band. MADOCA and CLAS keep the exact lookup,
  as MADOCALIB and CLASLIB do.
- **A carrier arc ends at a data gap.** A satellite back after more than
  45 s without a carrier phase starts a new ambiguity. Slip detection relied
  on the loss-of-lock flag and the geometry-free / Melbourne-Wubbena tests,
  which a receiver that aligns a new arc's phase with its code can pass: a
  satellite back after 12 h moved a 24 h static solution 0.25 m, and one
  that missed a single 30 s epoch at the elevation mask moved it 0.08 m.
- **No precise orbit outside the SP3 samples.** The orbit was extrapolated
  past the last sample, 1.4 m off after 15 minutes. An epoch more than 5 s
  outside a satellite's samples has no precise orbit: with a daily IGS final
  file (15-minute samples, last at 23:45) the last 15 minutes carry the
  code solution.
- **BeiDou with precise clocks uses B1I / B3I**, the pair those clocks
  refer to, for BDS-2 as well as BDS-3.
- **`--realtime` with RTCM SSR**: a satellite waits for its own first
  update, and the MADOCA L6D age counts the reception latency.
- **`gnss solve --combined`** applies `--demote-fixed-status-min-satellites`
  after combining the two passes.
- **RINEX 4 observation files** whose records end after the last value
  (trailing blanks removed) are read.
- **Browser GUI**: a failed run shows the tool's error message above the
  log, and the number of warnings next to the result. Post-process has a
  *Code biases (Bias-SINEX)* field for `--dcb`.

## Default behavior changes

Horizontal error, one hour, against the reference coordinate:

| Run | v0.6.0 | v0.6.1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OBE4 2023-08-17, GPS, IGS final orbits and clocks, static, 60 min | 0.21 m | 0.03 m |
| the same, kinematic, 60 min | 0.24 m | 0.04 m |
| JPL GDGPS RTCM SSR, Kamakura 2024-02-12, static, 60 min | 0.029 m | 0.039 m |
| the same, time to stay below 0.2 m | 38.6 min | 2.9 min |
| JPL GDGPS RTCM SSR, Kamakura 2025-08-21, static, 60 min | 0.057 m | 0.019 m |
| the same, time to stay below 0.2 m | 42.1 min | 8.7 min |
| PPP-B2b, Kamakura 2025-08-21, static, time to stay below 0.2 m | 18.2 min | 13.4 min |
| HAS SIS, Kamakura 2025-08-21, static, 60 min (horizontal / up) | 0.118 / -0.332 m | 0.139 / -0.753 m |
| HAS IDD, OBE4 2023-08-17, static, 60 min | 0.116 m | 0.176 m |
| the same, kinematic, 60 min | 0.305 m | 0.306 m |

- **PPP with `--sp3` / `--clk`, no SSR, no `--dcb`**: GPS satellites whose
  epoch has both `C1C` and `C1W` use the `C1W` range. Receivers without
  `C1W` are unchanged. The first minutes are noisier (OBE4 static at 5 min:
  0.26 → 0.47 m); the bias is gone.
- **PPP with `--ssr-rtcm`** (`standard` / `has-idd`): satellites of
  constellations the stream does not carry, and satellites in a gap of their
  corrections, are not used. `--ssr-rtcm-profile legacy` is unchanged.
  - The OBE4 HAS IDD static number gets worse. One GPS satellite had no
    correction for 29 minutes of that hour, and its broadcast clock had
    been offsetting other errors.
- **Static ionosphere-free PPP with SSR corrections** (RTCM SSR, HAS,
  PPP-B2b) uses the phase where the gate had dropped it. Kinematic results
  are equal to the millimetre on the lanes above; MADOCA and CLAS are
  byte-identical.
  - Static `--combined` on the JPL GDGPS hours stays below 0.08 m from the
    first epoch (0.17 to 0.19 m at 10 min in v0.6.0).
  - The height of the HAS SIS static run of 2025-08-21 gets worse (see the
    table). Kamakura is outside the HAS service area; the cause is not
    isolated.
- **`gnss ppp --dcb <OSB file>`** applies the biases. Before, such a file
  failed to load.
- **PPP with `--antex`**: results move by the phase centre variations, and
  with an antenna calibrated for GPS / GLONASS only also by the offsets now
  given to the other constellations (centimetres in the one-hour runs
  above, 0.1 m in height for a Galileo-only or BeiDou-only day).
- **PPP with `--sp3` / `--clk`, no SSR**:
  - BDS-2 satellites (C01-C16) are not used. On the one station-day
    checked they pulled the kinematic height 0.01 m with all four
    constellations and 0.08 m with GPS alone beside them; lowering their
    weight did not help and the cause is not found. BDS-3 is used.
  - Epochs after the last orbit sample carry the code solution (status 1)
    instead of a PPP solution from an extrapolated orbit.
- **Every PPP mode**: a satellite without a carrier phase for more than
  45 s starts a new ambiguity. At the default 15 degree elevation mask the
  reproduce lanes, MADOCA and CLAS give the same results as before.

Two new reproduce lanes check float PPP of whole station-days with precise
products against the stations' EPN daily coordinates:

- `mgex-ppp`: OBE4 2022-11-24 with CODE MGEX products (GPS, GLONASS,
  Galileo, BDS-3): static 0.014 m horizontal / 0.005 m vertical after 24 h,
  kinematic RMS 0.019 / 0.022 m after the first hour.
- `epn-ppp`: OBE4, WTZR, MATE and ONSA 2023-08-17 with IGS final products,
  everything in IGS20: static within 0.011 m horizontal / 0.017 m vertical,
  OBE4 kinematic RMS 0.041 / 0.035 m.

The OBE4 hour of `igs-final-ppp` is now gated at 0.10 m.

## Get started

Open the browser GUI and press **Run demo pipeline**:
<https://rsasaki0109.github.io/gnssplusplus-library/app/>. Natively:

```sh
docker run --rm -v "$PWD/output:/workspace/output" \
  ghcr.io/rsasaki0109/gnssplusplus-library:v0.6.1 \
  demo --output-dir /workspace/output/self-contained-demo
```

The demo is an offline plumbing check, not a field-accuracy claim.

## Accuracy and validation scope

- The OBE4 result with the IGS final products is one station-hour; its
  coordinate agrees with the EPN daily solution to 1 cm. The station-day
  lanes are five station-days in Europe on two dates. The Kamakura
  recordings have no `C1W` and cannot test that change.
- What remains on the OBE4 HAS IDD hour comes with the corrections: against
  the IGS final products the HAS clocks differ by a satellite-specific
  constant of up to 0.3 m once the P1-C1 biases are taken out, which a
  one-hour float solution cannot separate.
- The OBE4 and Kamakura data are development data, not holdouts. The
  cssrlib-data samples and the IGS / EPN products are used for tests only
  and never redistributed.
